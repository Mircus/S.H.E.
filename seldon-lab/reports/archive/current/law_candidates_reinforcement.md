# Law Candidates: Reinforcement

## Candidate regularity

Reinforcement appears to be structurally distinct from birth. In the current
DBLP slices it is better explained by continued activity and persistence
accumulation than by the birth-oriented aggregation score.

## Cross-venue evidence

Current cross-venue evidence comes from the same `SDM 2018--2024` and
`WSDM 2018--2024` slices.

- `SDM`: `2336` reinforcement events, best predictor `author_level_proxy`,
  mean Spearman `0.1938`
- `WSDM`: `4028` reinforcement events, best predictor `author_level_proxy`,
  mean Spearman `0.1680`

This means reinforcement currently looks more activity-heavy than birth and is
not yet captured best by the aggregation-birth score.

## Counterexamples

Some structurally coherent candidates with strong closure still fail to
reinforce when activity flattens or disappears. Conversely, some edge-seed
tracks reinforce without a dramatic closure jump.

## Confidence level

Low to moderate. The distinction from birth is stable across venues, but the
current rule is still descriptive rather than law-like.

## What remains unclear

- whether reinforcement should be decomposed into multiple subtypes
- whether boundary-role reduction is a genuine reinforcement marker
- how much reinforcement depends on author-level productivity versus local
  aggregation coherence
